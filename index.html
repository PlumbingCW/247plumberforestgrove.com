A pipe let go under the kitchen sink. The basement floor drain is gurgling. The water heater is dripping from the bottom and the floor is wet. When it happens, you want a person on the phone, not a menu. Forest Grove 24/7 Plumbing is a phone line staffed around the clock that sends licensed plumbers to homes and businesses in Forest Grove and nearby towns. What Happens When You Call

The person who answers is a dispatcher, and the first minute is mostly questions. Where are you, what is the plumbing doing, and is anything unsafe? Those answers decide whether this is a true emergency or a problem that can wait for a same-day appointment when one is available. We do not promise an arrival time on the phone, because traffic, weather and other calls change it, and a made-up number helps nobody.

  1. You call (503) 974-3329 and tell the dispatcher your address, the symptom and anything that has already been shut off.
  2. The dispatcher asks a few safety questions, including whether anyone smells gas or sees water near electrical outlets.
  3. A licensed plumber is sent to your address.
  4. The plumber looks at the problem in person, finds the cause and explains the repair options.
  5. You hear a clear price before work starts. If you say no, nothing is done and nothing is started.

That last step matters most. A plumber who looks at your pipes can tell you what needs to happen, what is optional and what can wait. You decide with the number in front of you. If the visit shows a bigger job than the phone call suggested, the price is explained again before anyone picks up a wrench.

What Counts as a Plumbing Emergency

Some problems can sit overnight. Others cannot. A slow drip under a bathroom sink, a toilet that runs after flushing or a faucet that sprays sideways can usually wait for a scheduled visit. The list below is different. These are the situations where waiting costs you flooring, drywall, belongings or safety.

  • Water running from a broken pipe or fitting that you cannot stop, even after closing the main valve at the house.
  • Sewage coming up through floor drains, tubs or toilets, which usually means the main drain or sewer lateral is blocked.
  • No hot water in freezing weather, especially if you also suspect a frozen pipe.
  • Water heater leaking steadily from the tank or the relief valve discharging hot water.
  • Water spreading toward electrical panels, outlets or a finished basement.

If you smell gas, do not call us first. Leave the building, then call NW Natural at 800-882-3377 or 911 from outside. We do not diagnose gas leaks over the phone.

Hard freezes are occasional here, not constant. NOAA 1991 to 2020 normals put the January average low at 32.6 F and December at 32.8 F, so a cold snap can arrive after weeks of mild weather. The pipes at risk are in crawlspaces, attics, garages and outside walls. Water expands as it freezes, and a split pipe often shows itself only when it thaws and starts leaking.

The Work We Handle, Grouped by Problem

Plumbing calls fall into a few families, and knowing which one yours belongs to speeds up the phone call. Slow sinks, backed-up tubs and sewer clogs belong with drain cleaning and, for stubborn grease or roots, hydro jetting. If a drain keeps clogging, a camera shows what is going on inside the line.

No hot water, a tank that leaks or a heater that rumbles points to the water heater side of the trade, from repair to replacement to a tankless install. Wet spots on a ceiling, a hissing wall or a water bill that jumped without explanation mean a leak, and finding it without opening everything up takes the right equipment.

Then there are the fixtures and the bigger projects. Toilets, faucets and garbage disposals are the everyday repairs. Whole-house repiping, new installations and commercial work are planned jobs where the visit starts with measuring and ends with a written price. Water and Sewer Realities in Forest Grove Houses

Forest Grove is the oldest city in Washington County, and the housing shows it. The Census Bureau's ACS 2020 to 2024 estimates put the median year built at 1982 across 9,094 housing units. About 48 percent of those homes, 4,376 units, went up before 1980. Of those, 1,030 date to 1939 or earlier. Older homes mean older drain lines, older shutoff valves and more chance of surprises behind walls.

The water side is cleaner news. The City owns the water utility and draws from its Clear Creek Watershed plus treated water from the Joint Water Commission. The City reports no lead or galvanized service lines needing replacement. For questions about hardness, call the City water treatment team at 503-992-3259, because the City reports do not publish a figure.

Sewer responsibility is where homeowners get caught. The City maintains collection lines smaller than 24 inches and all manholes, and Clean Water Services handles the trunk lines, lift stations and the Fernhill Treatment Facility. But the private sewer lateral, the pipe running from your house to the public sewer connection, is the property owner's responsibility under City Ordinance 52.03. If it clogs or cracks, the repair is yours to arrange.

Clean Water Services may help with structural defects where they occur within the public right of way, and a plumber's camera video is what they ask to see. A sag in the pipe is not a structural defect. That is one reason to get a camera inspection before a big repair. Read more about camera inspection of the sewer line before deciding.

How to Check Any Plumber's License

You should never have to take a plumber's word for it. Oregon lets you check yourself before anyone touches your pipes. Contractors can be looked up on the Construction Contractors Board site at search.ccb.state.or.us, and individual plumbers can be checked through the Building Codes Division license search. Both take a minute.

Look for an active license and a name that matches the person at your door. Ask the plumber who shows up to confirm the name they are working under. A real professional expects the question and answers it without hesitation. Apply the same check to us or to anyone else you consider calling.

Permits are part of the same conversation. Replacing a water heater, altering piping inside walls, ceilings or floors, and work on building sewers and water services all need a plumbing permit in Oregon. The City of Forest Grove Community Development office issues them at 503-992-3229, and the person doing the work pulls the permit.

How We Explain Price

Plumbing prices vary because plumbing jobs vary. A clogged kitchen drain and a collapsed sewer line are different animals. What we can promise is the order of events: the plumber looks first, you get a clear price before work starts, and the work begins only after you agree.

What is actually wrong

A flapper replacement and a failed valve behind the wall are both a running toilet on the phone. The diagnosis sets the price.

How hard it is to reach

A pipe under an open sink costs less labor than one inside a finished wall or under a slab.

Parts and materials

A standard tank heater and a tankless unit are very different purchases, and fixtures range widely.

Permits and inspections

Water heater replacement and concealed piping work need permits, which add steps and fees.

Timing

Calls after hours often carry an added emergency charge. Cost guides such as HomeAdvisor report roughly $100 to $300 for emergency call-out.

Homeowner's policies usually cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a pipe that bursts, but often exclude slow leaks and gradual wear. Ask your insurer before you start repairs, take photos of any damage and keep every receipt.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you really answer the phone at night and on weekends?

Yes. The line at (503) 974-3329 is open 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. A dispatcher answers, asks about the problem and sends a licensed plumber. We do not promise a specific arrival time, but you will hear a clear price before work starts.

What should I do before the plumber arrives?

Close the main shutoff valve at your house if water is running. Never turn it off at the city meter. Switch off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel safely, move valuables out of the way and take photos for your insurer. If you smell gas, leave and call 911 or NW Natural at 800-882-3377.

Is the sewer line my responsibility or the city's?

In Forest Grove, the private lateral from your house to the public sewer connection belongs to the property owner. The City maintains smaller collection lines and manholes, and Clean Water Services handles trunk lines. If you are not sure where the blockage is, a camera inspection shows it.

Do I need a permit for plumbing work?

Often yes. Water heater replacement and changes to piping inside walls, ceilings or floors need a permit in Oregon. Fixing a sink, toilet or faucet that is not concealed in a wall usually does not. The City of Forest Grove Community Development office, 503-992-3229, can confirm for your address.

How do I know your plumber is licensed?

Ask for the plumber's name and look it up. The Oregon Construction Contractors Board lists contractors at search.ccb.state.or.us, and the Building Codes Division has a license search for plumbers. You can do this before anyone enters your home, and it works for any plumber you consider.
