Emergency Commercial Plumbing in Forest Grove, OR

Business plumbing emergency? Stop the water and call us.

Call (503) 974-3329

When a restroom floods or a floor drain backs up during business hours, a shop, restaurant or office can lose a day's work in minutes. Our 24/7 dispatch takes business calls at any hour. Stop using the affected fixtures right now, close the building's main shutoff if water is running, keep customers out of wet areas, and call (503) 974-3329.

A commercial building is not a bigger house. It has more fixtures on one line, longer drain runs, shared systems and rules that depend on who owns what. We send licensed plumbers, offer same-day appointments when available for non-urgent work, and state the price clearly before the work starts.

After-Hours Emergency: When the Business Cannot Open Tomorrow

Some problems force a closure. Sewage rising in a floor drain or restroom, a failed water main inside the building, a broken water heater flooding a back room, or a gas smell all fall in that group. Gas comes first. Leave the building, call NW Natural at 800-882-3377 or 911 from outside, and do not return until cleared.

For water, close the main valve inside the building. For a sewage backup, stop flushing and running sinks, since every fixture on that line is feeding the same blockage. The City of Forest Grove keeps an after-hours sewer emergency line at 503-412-2444 for problems in the public sewer. A private lateral from the building to the public connection is the property owner's duty under City Ordinance 52.03.

Restaurants, Grease and Why Kitchen Lines Fail First

Fat, oil and grease cool as they travel down a pipe and cling to the walls. Over weeks, the opening narrows, and one busy Saturday can finish the job. A grease trap or interceptor is a tank that slows wastewater so grease floats to the top and can be pumped out before it reaches the sewer.

These tanks need regular pumping, and skipped service is a common reason a kitchen line backs up in the middle of dinner service. Clean Water Services asks everyone to keep grease out of drains, and a commercial kitchen cannot rely on that alone. Scrape plates dry, use strainers on floor drains, and keep a cleaning log. If the line is already slow, hydro jetting cuts grease off the pipe wall instead of just punching a hole through it.

Restrooms That Serve the Public

A public restroom is hard on plumbing. Many users flush many times a day, and the wrong things go down. Wipes are the worst. Clean Water Services asks that even "flushable" wipes, paper towels and mop refills stay out of toilets. A sign on the stall door helps more than a plumber's visit does.

Common service calls include flush valves that stick, urinal traps that clog, hands-free faucets with failing sensors, and supply stops that seize. Replacing a fixture that is not concealed in a wall does not need a permit, but altering piping inside walls does. Water-saving upgrades help too. The City of Forest Grove offers a $75 rebate on WaterSense toilets for customers on City water, so ask about eligibility before you buy.

Backflow Prevention and Annual Testing

A backflow preventer is a valve assembly that stops dirty water from flowing backward into the drinking supply. It protects fire sprinklers, irrigation, boilers and any connection where contamination could pull back. Many towns require periodic testing of these devices, and the rules vary by city.

North Plains, for example, says backflow devices must be tested at installation, yearly and after any repair, by a qualified tester. Cornelius runs a backflow testing program of its own. HomeGuide lists a backflow test at $30 to $300 per device. Check your city's program, then schedule the test before the due date, not after a notice.

Storm Drains, Roof Leaders and a Wet Winter

Forest Grove gets 46.72 inches of precipitation a year, with December averaging 8.72 inches and January 7.19. Parking lots, roof drains and yard catch basins clog with leaves and mud before the heaviest months. A drain that is clear in September can be blocked by December.

Clear the grates yourself with gloves and a rake, and do not reach into a catch basin. If water ponds against a building after rain, call. Ponding can mean a buried line has collapsed or filled with silt. A camera inspection shows what is in a line before anyone digs. See our sewer inspection page for how that works.

Tenant or Landlord: Who Handles the Repair

Your lease decides, so read it before arguing. In general terms, the building owner is responsible for the structure and shared systems, such as the main water line, the sewer lateral and plumbing inside walls. A tenant is often responsible for damage they cause and for fixtures inside the leased space. That is a general pattern and not a rule.

In an emergency, stop the damage first, then sort out cost. Tell the landlord or property manager in writing, keep photos, and keep the plumber's invoice and description of the cause. A plumber can tell you what failed. A plumber cannot tell you who owes the bill, and we do not decide that. If you need a plumber to look at it, call (503) 974-3329.

Older Buildings Along Pacific Avenue and Beyond

Historic Downtown Forest Grove, centered on Pacific Avenue and Main Street, was listed on the National Register in 2020. The Star Theatre building at 2028 Pacific Ave opened in 1912. Buildings of that age may have additions, patched lines and old cast-iron drains, which means a clog may involve more than a surface cleaning.

Altering piping inside walls, ceilings or floors needs a permit from the City Community Development office at 503-992-3229. Replacing a water heater does as well. Hillsboro, the county's largest city, has its own permitting office, so a business with locations in Hillsboro should expect a different process there. If a drain keeps backing up, drain cleaning clears the blockage and a camera shows the cause.

What Commercial Plumbing Costs and Why

Commercial jobs run on time and complexity. According to Angi, Portland plumbers run $45 to $200 per hour, with service call fees of $100 to $250 on top of that. A main sewer clog costs a national average of about $380 while most jobs fall from $187 to $572. Jetting costs more, often $600 to $1,400 per HomeGuide. Your price is explained before work starts.

A four-inch floor drain is not a half-inch supply, and larger pipe and heavier equipment cost more to work on.

Grease and buildup

After hours calls

Business property and interruption policies sometimes cover water damage and lost income, but exclusions are common, especially for slow leaks and sewer backups. Ask your agent before starting cleanup, and keep photos, invoices and the plumber's written findings.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you handle commercial plumbing emergencies at night or on weekends in Forest Grove?

Yes. Our 24/7 dispatch takes business calls nights and weekends. Call (503) 974-3329 and describe the problem. A licensed plumber is sent and the price is explained before work starts. No arrival time is promised. Close the building main for water, stop using affected fixtures and keep people away from wet floors.

Can you pump or clean a grease trap?

Grease trap and interceptor service is part of keeping kitchen lines running, and a plumber can clean the lines that feed it and check the connections. Pumping the tank itself may be a separate service, so describe your setup on the call and ask what a visit covers. Keep grease out of drains and follow Clean Water Services guidance.

Who is responsible for a sewer backup, my landlord or me?

It depends on your lease and where the blockage is. In Forest Grove the private lateral to the public connection is the property owner's duty. Inside the building, the lease usually decides. A plumber can find the cause. Report it to your landlord in writing and keep photos and the invoice.

Do I need a permit for commercial plumbing work?

Altering piping inside walls, ceilings or floors needs a permit, as does replacing a water heater or relocating plumbing. Simple fixture swaps that are not concealed in a wall do not. The City of Forest Grove Community Development office at 503-992-3229 handles those permits. Ask us before work starts if you are unsure.

How often does backflow equipment need testing?

Rules differ by city. North Plains requires testing at installation, yearly and after any repair. Cornelius runs a backflow testing program. HomeGuide lists tests at $30 to $300 per device. Ask your water provider or city what applies, then schedule the test before the due date.
