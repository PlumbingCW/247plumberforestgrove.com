Emergency Toilet Repair in Forest Grove, OR

Overflowing, leaking or running? Call for a same-day appointment when available.

Call (503) 974-3329

A toilet that is rising toward the rim, leaking across the floor or running without stopping will not wait for morning. We dispatch licensed plumbers 24/7. Right now, reach behind the bowl and turn the small oval-handled shutoff (the angle stop) clockwise, lift the tank lid and stop the flapper from lifting, and keep everyone out of the wet room.

Most toilet calls trace to a few cheap parts, and the rest trace to a drain problem the toilet is only showing you. Call (503) 974-3329 and tell us what the bowl is doing, what you can hear and whether other drains are slow. That one description helps us line up the right visit.

Overflowing Right Now: The First Two Minutes

If the bowl is about to spill, close the angle stop behind it. If it will not turn, take the lid off the tank and lift the float arm or ball so the tank stops refilling. If water is still spreading, close the main shutoff valve at the house. Never close the valve at the city meter. The City's rule is that customers do not touch it.

Call Public Works at 503-992-3258 if the house valve will not close, and the City will shut the water off at the meter. That call is free in business hours and carries a fee after hours. Do not flush again. Mop standing water with towels. If sewage, not just bowl water, is backing up, stop using every drain in the house.

Running Toilet: Flapper, Fill Valve and Chain

Inside the tank, a rubber flapper seals the hole at the bottom. When you flush, the handle lifts it, the tank drains into the bowl, and the flapper drops back down. The fill valve then refills the tank to a set level, shut off by a float. If the flapper is warped or the float is set too high, water keeps slipping past or running into the overflow tube.

The EPA's WaterSense program says to replace flappers at least every five years, and that the average household leaks more than 9,300 gallons of water a year. A toilet is a common source, in Cornelius homes as much as here. If the tank water level sits at the top of the overflow tube, adjust the float. If it hisses with the level correct, the fill valve is the likely culprit.

The Dye Test That Saves You a Guess

Drop a dye tablet or a few drops of food coloring into the tank, and do not flush. Wait ten minutes. If color shows up in the bowl, water is leaking past the flapper, which is the most common cause. EPA WaterSense recommends this test. The City of Forest Grove also hands out free dye tablets to customers on City water.

For a leak you cannot find, EPA also suggests reading your water meter before and after two hours of no water use. If the dial moves, something is running somewhere. A family of four using more than 12,000 gallons a month in a colder month may have a serious leak. If the toilet checks out and the meter still moves, a leak detection visit can trace it.

Leaking at the Base: Wax Ring and Bolts

A wax ring seals the toilet flange to the bowl outlet. When it fails, water or sewer gas escapes at the floor, often only during or after a flush. Loose closet bolts or a rocking toilet usually cause it. You may also see a stain on the ceiling of the room below, or soft flooring around the base.

Do not just caulk the base. A plumber pulls the toilet, inspects the flange and subfloor, replaces the ring and sets the toilet back square. If the floor has softened from a long leak, that needs fixing too. About 48 percent of Forest Grove homes were built before 1980, so worn flanges and tired subfloors are more likely.

Clogs, Plungers and When the Problem Is the Drain

A flange plunger, which has a rubber collar to seal the bowl outlet, solves most simple clogs. Push gently first to avoid a splash, then pump with steady strokes. A closet auger, a short cable made for toilets, reaches the trap without scratching the porcelain. Skip chemical drain cleaners, which are hard on pipes and on whoever works next.

If several fixtures back up, the problem is likely farther down, and drain cleaning is the next step. Clean Water Services asks residents not to flush wipes, including ones sold as flushable, or paper towels. The private sewer lateral from your house to the public sewer belongs to the owner, so sewer line cleaning is on the owner's side. The City's after-hours sewer emergency line, 503-412-2444, covers the public side.

Replacing the Toilet and the WaterSense Rebate

Some toilets are better replaced than repaired, especially cracked bowls, repeated clogs and older models that waste water. Under Oregon permit rules, repairing or replacing a toilet that is not concealed in a wall does not need a permit. A broader fixture upgrade falls under plumbing installation.

The City of Forest Grove offers a $75 rebate for WaterSense toilets rated at 1.28 gallons per flush or less. It is limited to two per household, for life, for customers on City water, and you must apply within 60 days of purchase. North Plains also lists a WaterSense toilet rebate, so ask that city for amounts. Check current terms before you buy.

What Toilet Repair Costs

Cost guides report valve or flapper work at about $70 to $150 plus a service call of roughly $50 to $200 (HomeGuide). Angi lists a running toilet repair in Portland at about $110 to $425. A wax ring job runs about $55 to $230. Those are guide figures, not our price. We give you a clear price before work starts.

Parts versus labor

A flapper is cheap. Time spent diagnosing a hidden leak is what drives the bill.

Wax ring and flange

Pulling and resetting the toilet takes longer than swapping a tank part.

Clog depth

A clog at the trap is quick. One further down needs an auger or cleaning the drain line.

Floor damage

Soft flooring or a corroded flange from a long leak adds repair work.

Time of the call

Cost guides say after hours visits often cost more, and the price is stated before work starts.

Insurance may cover sudden overflow damage to floors and ceilings but usually not the toilet repair or gradual leaks. Take photos, mop up and save your receipts. Ask your insurer what applies to your policy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you handle toilet repair emergencies at night or on weekends in Forest Grove?

Yes. Dispatch is 24/7, so a call after hours is answered. Call (503) 974-3329, describe what the toilet is doing, and we set up a visit. We do not promise an arrival time. We do give you a clear price before work starts.

How do I do the dye test to find a toilet leak?

Put a dye tablet or a few drops of food coloring in the tank and do not flush. Wait ten minutes. If color appears in the bowl, the flapper is leaking, according to EPA WaterSense. The City of Forest Grove gives out free dye tablets to customers on City water.

Why does my toilet keep running?

Usually a worn flapper, a flapper chain caught under the seal, or a fill valve or float set too high. Lift the tank lid and look. If water rises to the top of the overflow tube, adjust the float. If the flapper is soft or warped, replace it. WaterSense says at least every five years.

What do I do if the toilet is overflowing?

Shut the angle stop behind the bowl by turning it clockwise. If it will not move, lift the float in the tank so it stops filling, and close the main shutoff valve at the house if needed. Never close the city meter. Do not flush again, and call a licensed plumber.

Can I get a rebate for a new toilet?

The City of Forest Grove gives a $75 rebate for WaterSense toilets rated 1.28 gallons per flush or less. The limit is two per household, for life, and you must apply within 60 days of purchase. You also need to be on City water. Check current terms with the City before buying.
