Emergency Water Service Line Repair in Forest Grove, OR

Meter spinning or yard soaked? Close the house valve and call.

Call (503) 974-3329

If water is bubbling up in the yard, the meter dial is spinning with every tap closed, or the whole house has lost pressure, the buried line between the meter and your house may have failed, and that is an emergency. Close the main shutoff valve inside the house, not the valve at the city meter, then call (503) 974-3329. Our 24/7 dispatch gets a licensed plumber moving, and the price is explained before any digging begins.

This page is about the underground water pipe that feeds the house, not the plumbing inside it. It covers how to shut water off safely, who to call at the City, how plumbers find and fix a buried leak, and the permits and locates that come with digging.

Right Now: Shut Water Off at the House, Not the Meter

Find the main shutoff valve where the line enters the house. It is often in a basement, crawlspace, garage or utility closet, near the water heater or on a front wall. Turn it clockwise until it stops. Open a faucet at the lowest point to drain what is left, and check that the flow stops.

Forest Grove policy is that customers and contractors never turn the water off at the city meter. If the house valve is stuck or missing, call Public Works at 503-992-3258 and the City will shut it off. There is no fee during business hours and a fee after hours. If the meter or line breaks because someone closed it, repairs are at the homeowner's expense.

Once the water is off, you can still call at any hour. Nights and weekends are covered by dispatch, and a plumber can be sent same-day when the schedule allows.

Where Your Responsibility Begins and Ends

Water utilities in this area do not all draw the line in the same place. Cornelius and Hillsboro both say the owner is responsible from the meter to the building, and North Plains tells residents to call a licensed plumber for a leak between meter and home. Forest Grove has its own rules, and we do not guess at them.

Before you pay for a repair, call Public Works at 503-992-3258 and ask where the City's responsibility ends and yours begins on your lot. Write down the answer. A plumber also needs to know exactly where the meter sits.

The City reports no lead or galvanized service lines needing replacement, so repair here is about leaks and damage, not removing toxic pipe. If the break came from a freeze, burst pipe repair may be part of the same visit.

Signs a Buried Line Is Leaking

A service line leak does not show up in a sink. It shows up in the yard, the meter box or the water bill. Learn the signs, since a small leak can run for months before it surfaces.

Symptoms of a failing water service line.

According to the EPA, you can read the meter, use no water for two hours, then read it again. If the number moved, something is leaking. It also says nine percent of homes have leaks wasting 50 gallons a day or more.

Pinpointing a Leak Under the Yard

Digging blind is expensive. Plumbers start by isolating the line, closing valves one at a time to decide whether the leak is on the service line or inside the house. If it is outside, listening gear, a pressure test and sometimes tracer gas narrow the spot to a few feet. Our page on finding hidden leaks covers the equipment.

Cost guides do not give one figure for tracing a buried line, so ask for the locating price before any digging starts. Pinning down the spot first often saves more than it costs.

Trench or Trenchless: Choosing How the Line Gets Replaced

A trenched repair opens the ground along the pipe, from a small spot hole for one leak to a full run for replacement. It is the simplest method and gives the plumber a view of the soil and bedding. Trenchless methods work from two small pits and pull new pipe through, which helps when the line runs under a driveway, a patio or large roots.

Trenchless is not always possible. The route must be clear, and bends or old repairs can block it. A plumber should explain why one method fits your yard. If the leak is a single failed fitting, a short spot repair is often enough. If the pipes inside are failing too, see repiping a house.

Permits and 811 Before Anyone Digs

Oregon requires a permit for building sewers and water service, and the person doing the work, homeowner or contractor, pulls it. In Forest Grove the City's Community Development office at 1924 Council St issues plumbing permits, phone 503-992-3229. Call early, since a permit expires if work does not start within 180 days.

Always call 811 before digging so gas, electric and other lines get marked. Forest Grove Light and Power is city-owned and NW Natural serves gas, so lines may run close to your water pipe. Call even when the repair is urgent. Cornelius contracts building services from the Forest Grove Building Division, and North Plains permits through Washington County, so confirm the office that applies.

Water Service Line Repair Costs and Why They Vary

HomeGuide reports $50 to $200 per foot to replace a service line by trenching. Angi's Portland average for water service line replacement is $2,065 with a range from $1,084 to $3,207. A short spot repair costs far less than a full run. These are guide numbers, not our price. You hear ours before work starts.

Pressure Regulators and Backflow Devices Along the Line

Two devices often sit near where the line enters the house. A pressure-reducing valve, or PRV, lowers street pressure to a safe level. EPA says ideal home pressure is 45 to 60 psi and that most codes require a regulator where supply exceeds 80 psi. Angi lists PRV replacement at an average of $400 with a typical range of $200 to $700.

A backflow device keeps water from flowing backward into the supply. Irrigation lines and boilers commonly have one. HomeGuide lists testing at $30 to $300 per device. Ask about both while the line is open, and call (503) 974-3329 if you want them checked in the same visit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you handle water service line repair emergencies at night or on weekends in Forest Grove?

Yes. Dispatch runs 24/7, so call (503) 974-3329 any night or weekend. Shut the main valve inside the house first. You will get a licensed plumber and a price explained up front, and no one asks you to wait for office hours. No arrival time is promised.

Can I shut the water off at the city meter?

No. Forest Grove customers and contractors should never turn water off at the city meter. Use the main shutoff valve at the house. If it does not work, call Public Works at 503-992-3258. If the meter or line breaks because someone closed it, repairs are at the homeowner's expense.

Who owns the water service line in Forest Grove?

Ownership is set by the City, and we do not want to guess. Call Public Works at 503-992-3258 and ask where the City's part ends and yours begins on your property. That answer decides who pays, so get it before authorizing a dig.

How do I tell if the leak is in the yard or the house?

Shut the main valve at the house and read the meter. If the dial still moves, the leak is between the meter and the house valve. If it stops, the leak is inside. This quick check saves time on the phone, and a plumber can confirm with a pressure test.

Do I need a permit to replace a water service line?

Yes. Oregon requires a permit for water service work, and whoever does the work pulls it. The City of Forest Grove Community Development office issues them at 503-992-3229. Call 811 for utility locates before any digging, and remember that permits lapse after 180 days without work starting.
