Emergency Signs a Clog Cannot Wait Until Morning

Most clogs are a nuisance. A few are not. Water climbing toward the rim of a tub or toilet is a warning. So is more than one slow fixture at the same time, or gurgling from a drain you did not use. Any of these means the blockage is probably past the first few feet of pipe and getting worse. A bad clog at 11 p.m. still needs clearing tonight, and our after hours dispatch handles that.

Stop running water in any fixture that is draining slowly. Pull the washing machine plug if it drains into the same line. If anything coming up smells like sewage, keep kids and pets out of the room and call now.

What Clogs Drains in Older Forest Grove Houses

The housing numbers explain a lot. About 48 percent of Forest Grove homes were built before 1980, and roughly 1,030 date to 1939 or earlier. Older houses often have long, shallow-pitched branch lines, rougher pipe walls and more joints, all of which catch grease and lint sooner than a smooth modern run.

In the historic districts south of downtown, including Clark and Painter's Woods, one house can hold pipe from several decades. Where old pipe meets a newer addition, the joint is where debris likes to stop.

Snake or Jet: Matching the Tool to the Clog

A drain snake, also called a cable or auger, is a coiled steel line that a plumber feeds into the pipe to bore through or hook a clog. A jetter pushes high-pressure water through a hose to scour the pipe wall. The EPA calls jetting the most common hydraulic cleaning technique, and it is the better choice when grease or scale coats the pipe rather than plugging it in one spot.

Wipes, Grease and What Clean Water Services Asks of You

Clean Water Services asks everyone in the area not to flush wipes, even the kind sold as flushable, and not to flush paper towels or mop refills. Those materials do not break down like toilet paper. They catch on rough spots, snag on roots and knit into a mat that a cable cannot always pull free.

Grease is the other regular offender. It leaves the pan as a liquid, cools in the pipe and builds a waxy ring that narrows every year. CWS asks that you keep it out of drains and offers a Freeze the Grease kit by phone at 503-681-3678. Wiping pans with a paper towel before washing helps more than any additive.

What You Can Try Before You Call

  1. Remove the stopper or strainer and pull out any hair you can see.
  2. Use a cup plunger on a sink or tub, with the overflow opening blocked by a wet rag.
  3. Put a bucket under the sink trap, unscrew the slip nuts by hand and clean the trap out.

Skip bottled chemical openers. They can sit in a stopped pipe and burn skin or eyes when a plumber opens the line, and they do little against hair or wipes. If you already poured some in, tell us on the phone.

When a Repeat Clog Means Something Is Wrong in the Pipe

Two clogs in the same line within a year deserve a different question. Instead of asking how to clear it again, ask why it keeps stopping. A camera inspection of the line shows whether there is a sag where water pools, a cracked joint, or roots working in at a seam.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you handle drain cleaning emergencies at night or on weekends in Forest Grove?

Yes. Dispatch runs 24/7, so you can call (503) 974-3329 on a holiday or at 2 a.m. We send a licensed plumber and give you a clear price before work starts. We do not promise an arrival time, but we also do not make you wait for office hours when water is rising.

Can I use a store-bought drain snake myself?

For a bathroom sink or tub, a small hand snake is reasonable if you go slowly and stop at firm resistance. Do not force one into a toilet or a main line. You can scratch porcelain, kink the cable or push the clog deeper, which turns a small job into a bigger one.

Why does the same drain keep clogging?

Repeat clogs usually mean the pipe itself has a problem: grease lining the wall, a joint that sticks out, a sag that holds water, or roots. A cable opens a hole but does not fix the cause. A camera look after the next clog shows what is happening inside.

Is it safe to flush wipes labeled flushable?

Clean Water Services asks you not to flush them. Wipes, paper towels and mop refills do not break apart in the sewer the way toilet paper does. They tangle with grease and roots and can block your own pipe long before they reach the public sewer.

How do I know if the clog is in my pipe or the city sewer?

If only one fixture is slow, the clog is in your house. If every drain is slow or the lowest drain backs up, it may be your main line or the public sewer. The lateral is the owner's responsibility. A plumber can check from a cleanout and tell you which side the clog sits on.
