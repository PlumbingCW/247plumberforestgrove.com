Not every leak announces itself. Many show up only as a high water bill, a musty smell, or the sound of running water in a quiet house. Finding the exact spot without opening every wall is the whole job. We handle same-day appointments when available, and the price comes before the work does.

Water Where It Should Not Be Tonight

Active water damage gets handled in order. Stop the supply, protect what is dry, then find the source. The main shutoff valve is usually where the water line enters the house, often in a garage, crawlspace or utility room. Turn it clockwise, then open one low faucet to drain the pipes.

In Forest Grove, do not shut water off at the city meter. The City says customers and contractors never do that, and if the meter or line breaks afterward the repair falls to the owner. If the house valve will not close, call Public Works at 503-992-3258 and the City will close the supply. There is no fee in business hours and a fee after hours.

Ceiling stains that grow, bulging drywall or water dripping from a light fixture mean water is collecting overhead. Shut the main, stay out from under the sag and keep power off to that room.

Run the Two-Hour Meter Test Yourself

You can find out whether you have a leak before anyone arrives. According to EPA WaterSense, you should check the meter before and after two hours with no water use. It sorts leaks inside the house and beyond it from ordinary use.

  1. Turn off every faucet, appliance, ice maker and irrigation zone, and make sure nobody flushes.
  2. Open the meter lid and note the reading and whether the small leak indicator is turning.
  3. Wait two hours without using any water.
  4. Read the meter again. Any change means water is leaking somewhere.

A moving indicator with every fixture off means a leak. If the number stays put, the leak may be intermittent, like a toilet that refills on its own. Closing the main valve and watching the meter again helps separate a leak in the house from one on the service line. If the meter moved, call (503) 974-3329.

What Your Water Bill Is Telling You

Bills are slow evidence, but good evidence. EPA says a family of four using more than 12,000 gallons a month in a colder month may have serious leaks. A winter bill that jumps with no change in habits is worth a meter test.

Water pressure is another clue. EPA describes ideal home pressure as 45 to 60 psi, and pressure much lower than 45 can mean a leak. A gauge threaded onto a hose bibb takes a minute. In North Plains, the City says leaks between the meter and the home are a licensed plumber's job, while meter to street belongs to Public Works.

How Plumbers Pinpoint a Leak Without Demolition

The goal is to narrow the search before cutting anything. A plumber starts by isolating sections of pipe with valves and watching a gauge. A pressure test holds water in one branch and shows whether it loses pressure. The branch that drops is the one with the leak.

Acoustic methods come next. Sensitive listening gear picks up the hiss of pressurized water escaping a pipe, and a trained ear can mark a spot on a floor or wall. Moisture meters and thermal imaging help show where water has traveled. Drains are a different story, and a camera inspection suits those. We describe the method before using it.

Once the spot is marked, the choice is a small access hole, a patch, or a larger repair. A single failed fitting is a quick fix. A pipe that is pitted along its whole length may point toward replacing the piping instead of chasing leaks one at a time.

Slab Leaks and Old Pipes in Forest Grove Homes

Some houses with slab floors keep supply lines under the concrete, and a failing hot line can warm the floor above it. Angi puts slab leak detection at an average of $280 with a range of $150 to $400 and reports a plumber's flat leak diagnosis around $150. Repair is billed separately.

Forest Grove's housing spans a wide age range. The 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey shows a median year built of 1982, with about 48 percent of the 9,094 units built before 1980 and 1,030 in 1939 or earlier. Older houses mix copper, galvanized steel and later patches, and the joints between them leak first. The City reports no lead or galvanized service lines needing replacement, so any galvanized pipe in those homes is on the house side.

A leak on the line between the meter and the house is its own repair. See our page on water service line repair. For who owns what in Forest Grove, ask Public Works at 503-992-3258.

Quiet Leaks: Toilets, Heaters and Outdoor Lines

A toilet flapper that does not seal sends water down the drain all day. Drop a dye tablet or food coloring into the tank, wait 10 minutes, and color in the bowl means a leak. EPA suggests replacing flappers at least every five years. The City of Forest Grove offers dye tablets and a $75 rebate on WaterSense toilets. Our toilet repair page covers the fix.

Water heaters leak at the temperature and pressure valve, the drain valve and through the tank itself. EPA advises checking that valve at least once a year. For outdoor spigots, close the inside valve feeding the hose bibb, then see whether the meter still moves.

What Leak Tracing Costs and Why It Varies

Detection is priced by time and difficulty, not by a set menu. The cost guide figures above are only a yardstick. Our price is given before work starts, and we explain what method we plan to use.

Where the leak is

A visible fitting under a sink takes minutes. A line under a slab or behind tile takes longer to pin down.

Type of pipe

Copper, galvanized steel and plastic carry sound and pressure differently, so the method changes.

Access

Open crawlspaces help. Finished basements and tile floors mean more time and more repair afterward.

Number of leaks

Older systems often leak in more than one place, so the first find may not be the last.

Timing

Not every leak needs a midnight call. Water spraying, dripping through a ceiling, or running across a floor is an emergency, and so is a meter that keeps spinning with every fixture off. A damp cabinet or a slightly high bill can wait for a same-day appointment when available.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you handle leak detection emergencies at night or on weekends in Forest Grove?

Yes, dispatch runs 24/7 and takes calls nights and weekends. Call (503) 974-3329, describe what you see, and a licensed plumber is sent. The price is explained before work starts. No arrival time is promised, so shut the main valve at the house and turn off power to wet areas while you wait.

How do I know if I have a hidden leak?

Do the meter test. With everything off, read the meter, wait two hours with no water use, and read it again. Any movement means a leak. Other signs are a rising bill, a musty smell, damp or warm floor spots, low pressure, and the sound of water running when no one is using any.

Will you have to cut into my walls or floor?

Not at first. Plumbers narrow the location with pressure tests and acoustic listening before opening anything. Once the spot is marked, the opening is usually small. Some leaks, such as a failed line under a slab, need larger access, and the plumber explains the plan and the price before any cutting.

Do I need a permit to fix a hidden leak?

Repairing a faucet or toilet that is not concealed in a wall does not need one. Altering piping inside walls, ceilings or floors does, and so does emergency repair of leaking concealed piping when the new piping exceeds five feet. The City of Forest Grove Community Development office at 503-992-3229 handles permits.

Is a leak on the street side of my meter my problem?

That depends on which side of the meter the leak is on, and we do not guess who owns what. Call Public Works at 503-992-3258 and ask before spending money. Never shut the water off at the meter yourself, because a meter or line that breaks afterward becomes the owner's repair. Close the main valve at the house.
