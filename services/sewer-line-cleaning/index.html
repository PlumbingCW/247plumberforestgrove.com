Wastewater coming up through a shower drain, a basement floor drain or the lowest toilet in the house is an emergency. Stop running water everywhere, do not flush, keep children and pets out of the wet area, and call (503) 974-3329. Our 24/7 dispatch sends a licensed plumber to open the main line, and you get a clear price before work starts.

A main line backup is different from a slow sink. Every fixture in the house empties into one pipe, so when that pipe stops, the lowest opening takes the overflow. Below you will find what to do in the first few minutes, how a plumber finds the blockage, and how to tell whether the problem is yours or the public system's.

Sewage Is Backing Up Right Now: First Steps

Speed matters because every flush, shower and load of laundry adds more water to a line that cannot take it. Shut off appliances that drain and stay out of wet rooms. Raw sewage carries bacteria, so wear rubber gloves and boots if you must walk through it.

Do not use the toilets, sinks, shower or washer until a plumber has opened the line. Use the main shutoff valve at the house if water is still running into a flooded room. Never shut off water at the city meter. Call Public Works at 503-992-3258 if the house valve will not close.

Open windows if the smell is strong. If you see standing sewage, photograph it before any cleanup, since your insurer will want pictures. Then call, even after hours, and tell dispatch which fixtures are backing up and which are not.

Whose Pipe Is Stopped? Lateral, City Main and Trunk Line

Three sets of pipe sit between your toilet and the treatment plant. The private sewer lateral runs from the house all the way to the connection at the public sewer. In Forest Grove that lateral is the property owner's responsibility under City Ordinance 52.03.

The City maintains collection lines smaller than 24 inches and all manholes. Clean Water Services handles trunk lines, lift stations and the Fernhill Treatment Facility. If the blockage is out in the street main, neither a cable nor a jetter inside your property will fix it, which is why a plumber starts by figuring out where the stoppage is.

Find the Cleanout Before the Plumber Does

A cleanout is a capped pipe, usually four inches across, that gives direct access to the sewer line. It may sit outside near the foundation, in a crawlspace, in a basement or garage, or at the property line. Knowing where it is saves time, since the plumber can feed a cable straight in instead of pulling a toilet.

If you cannot find one, say so on the call. Older homes sometimes have none.

Why Main Lines Stop in Forest Grove Homes

Roots lead the list. About 48 percent of local homes were built before 1980, and older clay and cast iron lines have more joints for roots to find. A hair-thin root enters, snags paper and grows into a plug. Wet winters keep soil damp and roots hungry.

Grease, wipes and paper towels are the next group. CWS asks residents not to flush wipes, including ones sold as flushable, and not to pour grease down drains. Then there are structural causes: a sag where waste settles, a joint that has shifted, or a collapsed section. Those return no matter how often you clean, and a camera inspection of the sewer finds them.

How a Plumber Clears a Main Line

The usual first tool is a large cable machine with a cutting head sized for four-inch pipe. If the line is coated or has fine roots at many joints, the plumber follows with a high-pressure jetter, then runs a camera to confirm the pipe is clear.

A good plumber does not leave once the water drains. They flush the line to prove it moves. If it backs up again in seconds, the clog was not the whole story.

When the Cleaning Does Not Hold

If the line backs up again within a few months, a cleaning removed the symptom, not the cause. Ask for a recording of the pipe and a plain description of what was seen. If the camera finds a crack, an offset joint or roots entering at one seam, repairing the damaged section is often the lasting fix.

CWS may repair structural defects in a lateral inside the public right of way, including cracked pipe, offset joints and root intrusion. A sag is not a structural defect. A plumber, homeowner or realtor can send CWS a video to ask. Digging for repair means calling 811 first, and the plumber may need plumbing and erosion control permits.

Living With a Lateral You Own

Because the private lateral belongs to the owner, small habits matter. Keep wipes and grease out of the system, do not plant trees over the line and know where your cleanout is. Owners in Banks and North Plains are in the same position, with Clean Water Services handling sewer service.

Once the line is open, plan a follow-up camera look rather than wait for the next emergency. For small clogs that stop at one sink, our page on clearing household drains covers the lighter options. Frequently Asked Questions

Do you handle sewer line cleaning emergencies at night or on weekends in Forest Grove?

Yes. Dispatch is 24/7, so call (503) 974-3329 at any hour, including holidays. A licensed plumber is sent and you get a clear price before work starts. We do not promise an arrival time, but the call is answered and the job is scheduled around the clock.

What should I do first when sewage backs up?

Stop using every drain and toilet, keep people out of the wet area, and photograph it for your insurer. Use the main shutoff valve at the house if water is flooding a room, never the city meter. Then call a licensed plumber. Do not pour chemicals into the line.

Is the clog my responsibility or the city's?

The private lateral from the house to the public connection belongs to the owner. The City maintains collection lines under 24 inches and manholes. A plumber can run a cable or camera from your cleanout and tell you which side the stoppage is on, which tells you who to call.

Can tree roots be removed for good?

A cutting head clears roots from the pipe, but the tree keeps growing and the opening stays. Roots often return in a year or two. A lasting fix usually means sealing or replacing the joint. Ask for a camera recording so you can see exactly where they are entering.

Why does the lowest drain in the house back up first?

Sewage follows gravity, so when the main is blocked the water rises through the lowest opening, often a basement floor drain or a ground-floor tub. If that drain overflows while upstairs fixtures still work, the blockage is usually in the main line or the lateral, not in a single fixture trap.
