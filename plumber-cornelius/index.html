What Makes a Cornelius Call Different

The 2020 Census counted 12,694 people in Cornelius, which incorporated in 1893. The railroad tracks south of Baseline Street are the reason the town exists.

The housing numbers tell the plumbing story. Median year built is 1992 across 4,949 homes. About 80 percent went up after 1970, and the 1990s were the peak decade with 1,223 homes. Only about 440 homes predate 1960, and just 105 were built in 1939 or earlier. So we see two kinds of call here, not one.

Old-core calls are about drain lines and supply piping that have had decades to wear, shift or collect roots. The 1990s calls are about appliances and fittings reaching the end of their normal life. A storage water heater lasts roughly 10 to 15 years, according to the US Department of Energy, so a house from the 1990s is likely on its second or third tank by now.

Calls That Come Out of Cornelius Homes

Slow or backed-up drains lead the list. A kitchen line that gurgles, a tub that drains slowly, and a toilet that rises when the washer empties usually point to a clog farther down than a plunger reaches. Cost guides such as Angi put snaking at about $100 to $250. If that does not clear it, a plumber checks the main with a camera. That is the work behind clearing a clogged drain.

Water heaters come next. A pool of water under the tank, a rumbling noise, or lukewarm showers can mean a failed element, a bad thermostat or a tank that has simply aged out. Cost guides list water heater repair in Portland from $266 to $1,114. Age decides repair versus replace, and that is the first question in water heater repair.

Toilets that run on and off are the quiet water wasters. EPA WaterSense says to drop a dye tablet or food coloring in the tank, wait 10 minutes, and look for color in the bowl. Color means a leak, often the flapper, which should be replaced at least every five years. A flapper swap is simple toilet repair, and HomeGuide lists valve or flapper work at $70 to $150 plus a service call.

Before We Arrive: What to Do at Home in Cornelius

Close the house valve, leave the meter

Shut the main valve where the line enters your house. Do not touch the meter. In Cornelius the owner is responsible from the meter to the house, so leave the meter to the city.

Stop the flow into a backed-up drain

If sewage is rising, stop flushing, skip the dishwasher and washer, and keep people away from the wet floor. Chemical drain cleaner can splash on whoever opens the line next, so leave it on the shelf.

Smell gas? Go outside first

Do not flip switches or look for the source. Leave the building, then call NW Natural at 800-882-3377 or 911 from outside. We do not diagnose gas leaks on the phone.

Frozen pipe or cold snap

The City advises a steady drip at the faucet farthest from the meter, heat no lower than 55 degrees, and never an open flame to thaw a pipe. If one splits, the plumber handles burst pipe repair after you close the house valve.

Who Owns What: Water, Sewer and Permits

Cornelius Public Works runs the water system, but the water itself is bought from the City of Hillsboro. The city purchased 462 million gallons in 2025. No lead service lines have been reported. Cornelius publishes no hardness figure, so we will not guess one. If water hardness matters for an appliance, ask Public Works.

The dividing line matters in an emergency. The city is responsible for a frozen main or meter, but not for pipes inside the home. From the meter to the house, the pipe is yours.

For sewage, the city maintains the main and Clean Water Services treats what it carries. Your side of the connection belongs to you, so a backup in your own line is a private repair, while a problem in the main is the city's to fix. A camera run tells a plumber which side the blockage is on.

Permits are handled by the Forest Grove Building Division, which Cornelius contracts for building services. Replacing a water heater needs a permit under Oregon rules, and so does altering piping inside walls, ceilings or floors. Swapping a toilet or faucet that is not concealed in a wall does not. Start with the Forest Grove Community Development office at 503-992-3229 if you are unsure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is emergency service available in Cornelius?

Yes. Dispatch runs 24/7, including nights and weekends. Call (503) 974-3329, tell us what is happening, and a licensed plumber is sent. You hear a clear price before work starts. We do not promise an arrival time on the phone. While you wait, close the main shutoff valve at the house and stop using any backed-up drain.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Cornelius?

Yes. Cornelius is 97113 for about 97 percent of its area. Small parts of the city are addressed in 97123 and 97124, both of which are Hillsboro ZIPs. Cascadia Park, for example, is addressed in 97124. Give us your street address and we will confirm you are in town.

Who pulls the permit for a water heater replacement in Cornelius?

The person doing the work gets the permit, whether that is a homeowner or a contractor. Cornelius contracts its building services from the Forest Grove Building Division. Replacing a water heater needs one, and the water heater must also be anchored or strapped. Ask the plumber before work begins who is pulling it.

Is a sewer backup the city's problem or mine?

It depends where the blockage sits. The city maintains the sewer main, and you maintain your own side of the connection. If sewage is backing up inside the house, the clog is usually in your line. A camera inspection shows which side it is on. Cost guides list inspections at roughly $125 to $1,000.

What should I do first if a pipe freezes in Cornelius?

Shut the main valve at the house, open the faucet the frozen pipe feeds, and warm the area gently with a hair dryer or heating pad. Never use a torch or open flame. The city handles a frozen main or meter, but pipes inside your home are yours. Call us if the pipe has split or you cannot find it.
