Before We Arrive: Steps to Take at Your Banks Property

Find the right shutoff

On city water, close the main valve where the line enters the house. On a private well, switch off the pump breaker as well so it does not keep running against a broken pipe.

Protect what is dry

Move rugs, boxes and electronics off the floor. Put a bucket under a dripping fitting and a towel dam at a doorway.

Stop using the drains

If sewage is backing up, no sinks, showers or flushes until we look. On septic, extra water can make a full tank worse.

Have the details ready

Know if you have city water or a well, sewer or septic, and roughly where the shutoff and the water heater are.

Common calls by property type

Banks is not a place of dense subdivisions. Census counts show homes rising only from 580 in 2010 to 617 in 2020, so the housing stock changed little. That is why we sort calls by how a property is plumbed, not by a neighborhood name. We have no year-built data for Banks, so we ask about the pipe material on the phone.

In-town homes on city water and sewer

These calls look like most small-town work. Clogged kitchen lines, a failing water heater element or tank, a leaking supply line under a sink. Roots in the lateral are a real possibility on any property with mature trees. The lateral is the pipe from the house to the public sewer connection, and it belongs to the owner.

Rural lots on a well and septic

Here the plumbing starts at a pump, a pressure tank and a pressure switch. Short cycling, sputtering faucets and a pump that never shuts off are common. Slow drains in several fixtures at once can point to a tank or drainfield problem, which is a septic matter and may need a septic specialist as well.

Properties near the trail and the wetlands

The Killin Wetlands are peat soil wetlands, and ground that stays wet can hide a slow underground leak. A soft spot in a yard, or a meter that moves when no one is home, is a clue worth a leak locating visit.

Water and drain rules to know

The City of Banks Water Department draws on springs above the West Fork of Dairy Creek, called Green Mountain Springs, and on deep wells near Banks Road in Columbia Basalt. The wells carry more of the load in the dry summer. The city treats the water by filtration and disinfection, and all service lines are non-lead. We will not quote a hardness number because the city has not published one.

According to its Water Curtailment Plan, the city limits water use in three stages, with fines up to $500. Large demands such as filling a pool or pond must be approved by the city first. The city also renewed a development moratorium for a documented water supply shortage in resolutions between 2018 and 2022. Its current status is unknown to us, so ask the city before planning new work that adds a connection.

Permits depend on the job. Plumbing permits here go through Washington County, and land use approval from the city comes first. Septic work, new or repair, goes through Washington County Environmental Health. If a water line outside the house is leaking, a service line repair may need a permit and an 811 call before anyone digs.

Private well owners carry the responsibility for their own supply. Oregon DEQ recommends testing a private well every one to two years. A plumber can fix a pump or a pipe, but the test is how you learn what is in the water.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is emergency service available in Banks?

Yes. Our dispatch line is answered 24/7, day or night, including weekends. A licensed plumber is sent from Forest Grove, about 6 miles away via Oregon Route 47. We give a clear price before work starts and do not promise an arrival time. While you wait, close the main valve at the house and keep clear of standing water.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Banks?

Banks has one primary ZIP, 97106. The rural ZIPs 97109 and 97125 also list Banks as an alias, so a Banks address can carry any of the three. We serve all of them. If you are not sure which ZIP applies, give the dispatcher your street address and cross road.

Do you work on private wells and septic systems around Banks?

We repair the plumbing side of a well, such as the pressure tank, switch, and the pipes into the house. Septic permits and onsite sewage work go through Washington County Environmental Health. A septic tank or drainfield problem may need a specialist along with a plumber. Tell us about it on the call.

Should I use chemical drain cleaner on a Banks septic system?

We advise against it. Chemical openers can damage old pipes and hurt a septic tank's balance. A cable or a jetter clears a clog without leaving harsh chemicals in the line. Also keep wipes, paper towels and grease out of drains. Clean Water Services asks the same of sewer customers.
