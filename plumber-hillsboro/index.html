What Runs Under Hillsboro Streets and Into Your House

Hillsboro water comes from the upper Tualatin River, treated at the Joint Water Commission plant, with water from Barney and Scoggins reservoirs added in summer. The city's 2024 report, using 2023 data, lists hardness at 26.1 to 31.9 ppm, which is 1.52 to 1.86 grains per gallon. That is very soft water. No lead service lines exist in the city's service area.

The city's responsibility runs from the main to the meter. From the meter to the building, the pipe belongs to the owner, and so does any repair on that stretch. Sewer works differently. The city and Clean Water Services share the system, but the lateral from your house to the public sewer is the property owner's duty.

That lateral count is large. Hillsboro has about 30,000 of them, and since 2021 the city has run a Lateral Accuracy Project to mark where they actually are. If a plumber needs to dig or bore on your property, call 811 first so utilities are located.

Soft water has practical effects. Scale builds slowly in a tank, on aerators and inside a tankless unit. The US Department of Energy notes that tankless heaters scale in hard water and need flushing with descaling solution, so hard-water towns need that more often than this one. Follow the manual either way.

Emergencies in Hillsboro That Cannot Wait

Call right away for water you cannot stop, sewage backing into a tub or floor drain, a leak near the electrical panel, or no hot water in freezing weather. Gas is different. If you smell gas, leave the building and call NW Natural at 800-882-3377 or 911 from outside. We do not diagnose gas leaks over the phone.

Water pouring out? Turn the main shutoff valve at the house, then open a faucet at the lowest level to drain the pipes. Do not dig into the meter box. Repairs on the customer's side of the meter fall to the customer.

Cold snaps are the other regular trigger. The city's advice is to keep the house at 55 degrees or warmer, let faucets trickle, and never thaw a pipe with a torch or open flame. A plumber can thaw a frozen line for a flat fee that cost guides such as Angi and HomeAdvisor put around $100.

Before We Arrive: What to Do at Your Hillsboro Property

Know your main shutoff

Find the main water valve before you need it. Most are where the line enters the house, often in a garage, crawlspace or utility closet. Turn it clockwise until it stops, then open the lowest faucet.

Photograph, then protect

Photograph the damage, then move rugs, boxes and electronics off the floor. A wet vac or buckets buy time but do not fix the source.

Free the work area

Clear a path to the water heater, the cleanout or the sink cabinet. Tell dispatch if the property is a commercial space with a keyholder or a locked mechanical room.

Keep the heat on

In cold weather, leave the thermostat at 55 or above and open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls. Warm air reaching the pipes can keep a frozen one from splitting.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is emergency service available in Hillsboro?

Yes. Dispatch is 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Call (503) 974-3329, describe what is happening, and a licensed plumber is sent. Price is stated clearly before work starts. We do not promise a time by phone. Close the house shutoff valve while you wait if water is running.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Hillsboro?

Yes. Hillsboro uses 97124, the larger ZIP that holds the courthouse and airport, and 97123, which is also a Hillsboro ZIP. Some of the city's eastern edge is addressed in 97006. The ZIPs overlap with neighboring towns, so we go by the street address, not the ZIP printed on your mail.

Does very soft water mean I can skip water heater flushing?

No. Soft water slows scale, but the Department of Energy still lists routine tank care. Flush a quart every three months, test the temperature and pressure valve every six months, and inspect the anode rod every three to four years. Tankless units still need descaling per the manual.

Who is responsible for my sewer lateral in Hillsboro?

The property owner is, from the house all the way to the connection at the public sewer. The city and Clean Water Services handle the public system. If a camera shows a problem, the owner arranges the repair. A permit and utility locates by calling 811 may be needed before digging.

Do I need a permit to replace a water heater in Hillsboro?

Yes. Hillsboro issues its own plumbing permits through the Development Services and Permitting Center at 150 E Main St, and a permit is required to replace a water heater. The heater must also be anchored or strapped. Ask your plumber who is pulling the permit before work starts.
