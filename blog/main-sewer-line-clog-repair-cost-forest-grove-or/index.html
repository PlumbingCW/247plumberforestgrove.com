Main Sewer Line Clog Repair Cost in Forest Grove, OR

By the Forest Grove 24/7 Plumbing team. Updated October 15, 2026.

Clearing a main line usually costs a few hundred dollars, and jetting, access and a Portland-area market can push it higher.

Call (503) 974-3329

Cost guides put main sewer line clog clearing in the low hundreds to about $1,000 and sometimes more. Angi reports a national average of $380 and a Portland average of $994. HomeGuide lists $175 to $800 for a main line and $600 to $1,400 when jetting is needed.

Those are published guide numbers, not a quote. The bigger question for most owners is who pays, because in Forest Grove the line from your house to the public sewer is yours. Here is what moves the price, how the ownership split works, and who to call first.

Snaking, Jetting and the Camera: Three Different Jobs

A cable machine, often called snaking, bores through a soft blockage. It is the cheapest tool and works well for paper and food buildup. It can also punch a hole through a root mass and leave most of it in place, which is why clogs sometimes come back in a few weeks.

Jetting aims a high-pressure water stream at the blockage. The EPA calls it the most common hydraulic cleaning technique. It scours grease and silt off the pipe wall instead of just poking a hole. Angi puts the average hydro jetting job at $475 nationally and $501 in Portland, with a Portland range of $369 to $632.

A camera inspection sends a small lens down the line. The EPA calls this the most commonly used technique to look inside a sewer. HomeGuide lists $125 to $500 and HomeAdvisor reports higher averages, so plan on roughly $125 to $1,000 depending on access and the report you get. It is worth paying for when the clog repeats.

Signs the Blockage Is in the Main, Not a Branch

A clog in one sink or one toilet is a branch problem. A main line clog shows up in several places at once, and usually at the lowest point in the house first.

  • A basement or lowest-floor drain bubbles or backs up when you run the washer or flush.
  • Multiple fixtures drain slowly at the same time.
  • A toilet gurgles when the shower runs.
  • Sewage smell in the yard or near a cleanout cap.
  • Water in the tub rising after a flush.

If you see sewage coming up, stop using water. Do not run dishwashers or washers, and shut off the main valve at the house if water keeps flowing into the drain. Never shut it at the city meter.

Why One Clog Costs More Than Another

Four things shift the final bill. The first is access. A clear cleanout near the house lets the plumber start fast. If there is no cleanout, a toilet may have to be pulled to reach the line, which adds labor.

The second is what is in the pipe. Paper and grease clear quickly. Tree roots and collapsed or offset joints do not. The third is how deep the clog is and how long the line runs, since a longer run takes more cable or hose. The fourth is timing, because nights, weekends and holidays usually cost more.

Portland-area plumbers charge $45 to $200 per hour, with service call fees of $100 to $250, according to Angi. Those are Portland-area figures, so treat them as a guide only.

Your Side of the Line and the City's Side

Sewer responsibility is split. In Forest Grove the private sewer lateral, from the house all the way to the connection at the public sewer, is the property owner's duty under City Ordinance 52.03. A clog in that stretch is yours to clear and repair.

The City maintains collection lines smaller than 24 inches and all manholes. Clean Water Services takes the trunk lines, the lift stations and the Fernhill Treatment Facility. If the camera shows the blockage is out in the public main, that stretch is maintained by the City or Clean Water Services rather than by the owner.

There is one more wrinkle. CWS may repair structural defects in a lateral, such as cracked or broken pipe, open or offset joints and root intrusion, where they occur within the public right of way. A plumber, homeowner or realtor can send CWS a video to request help. A sag, sometimes called a belly, is not counted as a structural defect.

Who to Call and in What Order

  1. Stop water use and keep everyone off the fixtures that are backing up.
  2. Call a licensed plumber at (503) 974-3329 to clear the line and run a camera if needed.
  3. If the camera shows the blockage is in the public main, ask the plumber to share the footage and call the City after-hours sewer emergency line at 503-412-2444.
  4. If it looks like a structural defect in the right of way, CWS Field Operations is at 503-547-8100.
  5. For any lateral digging, a plumber may need a plumbing permit and an erosion control permit, plus utility locates through 811.

The City's Community Development office at 1924 Council St issues building and plumbing permits, and its number is 503-992-3229.

Keeping the Main Line from Clogging Again

Clean Water Services asks everyone not to flush wipes, including ones labeled flushable, or paper towels and mop refills. They also ask that grease stay out of drains. For a grease-collection kit, the number is 503-681-3678.

Families in Hillsboro have a similar duty, since owners there are responsible for laterals. Across Cornelius, the city maintains the main and the owner handles their own side, as explained on our Cornelius service page.

A repeat clog every few months calls for more than another snaking. A hydro jetting service or a camera look can find the cause before you pay for the fifth visit.

Forest Grove Note: Lateral, Main and Ordinance 52.03

In Forest Grove, the lateral belongs to the owner under City Ordinance 52.03. That means the blockage between your foundation and the public connection is your cost, while the City cares for smaller collection lines and manholes. The City's after-hours sewer line is 503-412-2444.

Forest Grove also has older housing, with about 48 percent of homes built before 1980 and 1,030 built in 1939 or earlier. Older lateral materials and more years of root growth are reasons a camera look is often worth doing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who pays for a main sewer clog in Forest Grove?

The property owner pays for clogs and repairs in the private lateral, from the house to the public sewer connection, under City Ordinance 52.03. The City maintains collection lines smaller than 24 inches. If a camera shows the problem is in the public main, call the City at 503-412-2444.

How much does it cost to clear a main sewer line?

Cost guides report $175 to $800 from HomeGuide, or $600 to $1,400 when jetting is needed. Angi's national average is $380 and its Portland average is $994. Your price depends on access, what is in the pipe and timing, and it is named before any work begins.

Is a camera inspection worth the extra cost?

Often yes, when a clog returns. HomeGuide lists $125 to $500 and HomeAdvisor reports higher averages, so expect roughly $125 to $1,000. The camera shows whether you have roots, a crack or a sag, which decides whether clearing is enough.

Can I clear a main sewer line myself?

A small hand auger can handle simple clogs at a cleanout, but main lines are risky for beginners. A cable can snag roots, damage old clay pipe or push sewage into the house. If water backs up in more than one fixture, call (503) 974-3329.
