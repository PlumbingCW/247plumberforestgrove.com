How to Flush Sediment From a Water Heater

By the Forest Grove 24/7 Plumbing team. Updated October 15, 2026.

A short, regular flush keeps a tank quieter, cleaner and working harder for longer.

Call (503) 974-3329

To flush sediment from a water heater in Forest Grove, shut off the power or gas and close the cold water inlet. Then connect a hose to the drain valve, open a hot tap upstairs, and drain the tank until the water runs clear. Close the valve, refill the tank, and only then restore power or gas. The whole job takes about an hour.

How often depends on who you ask. According to the US Department of Energy, you should flush a quart of water every three months, and EPA WaterSense says to flush the whole heater once a year. Either habit beats never doing it.

What settles at the bottom of a tank

Heating water drives dissolved minerals out of solution, and they sink to the floor of the tank as a gritty layer. That layer sits between the burner or element and the water, so the heater works harder to do the same job. You may hear popping or rumbling as steam bubbles push through it. Hot water may run out sooner.

In a gas heater the burner sits under that layer, which can overheat the steel. In an electric heater the lower element can get buried and burn out. Neither failure shows up on a schedule. Sediment is a quiet cost that builds until something gives.

Amounts vary with water chemistry. Hillsboro's 2024 report lists hardness of 26.1 to 31.9 ppm, or 1.52 to 1.86 grains per gallon, which is very soft water. Hillsboro heaters have less mineral to drop than heaters in a hard-water town. Rust and scale flakes from old pipes can still collect.

A maintenance calendar you can keep

DOE and Oak Ridge National Laboratory publish simple tank maintenance intervals. Most of them take five minutes. Put them on a calendar you will see, like the same weekend you change smoke detector batteries.

Flushing a storage tank in order

You need a garden hose, a bucket for a first test draw, a screwdriver and a spot where hot water can drain safely, like a floor drain or the yard. Pick a time when nobody needs hot water for a few hours.

  1. Turn the thermostat to its lowest setting or the gas control to pilot. On an electric heater, switch off the breaker so the elements never run dry.
  2. Close the cold water valve at the top of the heater.
  3. Open a hot tap in the kitchen or a tub, which lets air into the system.
  4. Attach the hose to the drain valve at the bottom and run the other end to a drain or outdoors.
  5. Open the drain valve. The first water is hot, so keep skin and pets clear.
  6. Let the tank empty. Cloudy or gritty water at the start is normal.
  7. Open the cold inlet briefly a few times to stir the bottom, then drain again until the water runs clear.
  8. Close the drain valve, remove the hose, and open the cold inlet.
  9. Leave the hot tap open until water flows steadily with no sputtering, then close it. Restore the breaker or gas control only after that.

Flushing a heater that has never been drained can disturb a hard layer and clog the drain valve. If the water only trickles, stop and call (503) 974-3329 rather than forcing it.

Testing the relief valve

The temperature and pressure relief valve is the safety valve on the side or top of the tank. If heat or pressure runs too high, it opens and sends water down the discharge pipe. DOE says to check it at least every six months, and EPA WaterSense says at least once a year.

Place a bucket under the end of the discharge pipe. Lift the lever for a few seconds and let it snap shut. Water should flow out and stop when you release the handle. Wear gloves and stand to the side, because the water is scalding. If it keeps dripping afterward, or nothing comes out, the valve needs replacing.

Never cap or plug the discharge pipe. Relief valve termination is part of the code items when a heater is replaced, which is why a new line to a safe spot is part of a proper install.

The anode rod, the part that gets used up

A magnesium or aluminum rod hangs inside the tank. It corrodes in place of the steel, which is why it is called a sacrificial anode. DOE and ORNL say to inspect it every three to four years. Once the rod is mostly gone, the tank wall is the next thing to rust.

Pull it by unscrewing the hex head at the top of the tank, after the cold inlet is closed, a few gallons are drained and the power or gas is off. Bare steel wire showing along the length, or pitting, means replace it. Rods can seize in place, and some need an impact wrench, so many homeowners leave this step to a plumber during a water heater repair visit.

Tankless heaters need descaling instead

A tankless unit has no tank to flush, but it still has a heat exchanger that scales up. DOE notes that tankless heaters scale in hard water, and the exchanger should be flushed with a descaling solution as the manual directs. Follow the manual closely because solutions and ports vary.

Tankless homes often have service valves on the cold and hot lines so a small pump and hoses can circulate solution through the unit. If those valves were never installed, a plumber can add them. Watch for error codes, lukewarm water, or a drop in flow, which can signal scale.

Why your neighbor's sediment looks different

Two houses on the same street can show different results because of pipe age, usage and how long the heater has run. Cornelius buys its water from Hillsboro, though Cornelius does not publish a hardness figure of its own, so a flush is the better way to see what your tank collects.

The test draw tells you what to do. If a quart of water comes out clear for several quarters in a row, you can stretch the schedule. If you see grit or flakes, shorten it. Either way, write down what you see.

Forest Grove's water comes from the City-owned utility, which draws on the Clear Creek Watershed and treated Joint Water Commission water. Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How hard is the water in Forest Grove and does it change how often I flush?

The City of Forest Grove's reports do not list hardness, so there is no figure to quote. To find out, call the City water treatment team at 503-992-3259. Flush on the DOE schedule regardless, and let the grit you see in a test draw tell you whether to shorten or stretch it.

Can flushing damage an old water heater?

It can expose a problem that was already there. A heater that has gone years without a flush may have a hard layer of sediment and a drain valve that clogs. If it leaks at the valve afterward, the valve itself was worn. Heaters near 10 to 15 years deserve a plumber's eye first.

Do I need to flush a heat pump or tankless heater the same way?

Tankless units are flushed with a descaling solution through service valves, as the manual directs. A heat pump water heater has a tank, so it is flushed like any tank. Check the manual for its own steps, or have a plumber do the job.

What if the water stays cloudy or brown after flushing?

A few minutes of cloudiness is normal, but brown or rusty water that keeps coming points to a rusting tank or rusting supply pipes. Check the cold water too. If only hot water is discolored and the heater is old, the anode may be spent and the tank may be failing. Call (503) 974-3329 for an inspection.
