Garbage Disposal Repair in Forest Grove, OR: What You Can Fix Yourself

By the Forest Grove 24/7 Plumbing team. Updated October 15, 2026.

A humming, silent or dripping disposal usually has a simple cause, and most jams clear with a hex key and a reset button.

Call (503) 974-3329

If your disposal hums but the blades will not turn, it is jammed. Unplug it or switch off its breaker, put a hex key in the socket at the bottom center, and work it back and forth until the motor spins free. Then press the red reset button. That clears most jams.

A leak is a different problem, and a dead silent unit with no hum points to power or the reset. Below are the symptoms, the safe order for checking them, typical costs, and the point where you should stop and call a plumber.

What the Sound Under Your Sink Is Telling You

A disposal is a small motor driving a spinning plate with blunt lugs on it. It does not have knife blades. The lugs fling food against a grind ring, and water flushes the scraps down. The sound it makes narrows down the fault quickly.

Freeing a Jam With the Hex Key

Most disposals have a small hex socket in the center of the underside, usually sized for a quarter-inch wrench that often ships taped to the unit or sits in the drawer from the day it was installed.

  1. Switch the disposal off at the wall switch, then unplug it from the outlet under the sink or turn off its breaker. Do this every time, even if you only plan to look.
  2. Shine a flashlight down the drain. Never put your fingers in. Use tongs or long pliers to pull out anything you can see.
  3. Insert the hex key into the socket underneath and turn it back and forth. You will feel it bind, then give. Keep going until a full turn is easy.
  4. Run cold water, restore power, and press the red reset button on the bottom of the housing.
  5. Flip the switch with water running. If it hums again, stop. There is something still wedged in there or the motor is weak.

If the hex key will not turn at all, do not force it with a bigger tool. A seized motor needs a closer look.

The Reset Button Is a Thermal Switch, Not a Fuse

That red button is an overload protector. When the motor works too hard against a jam or overheats, the switch pops out to protect the windings. If it will not stay in, wait ten minutes for the motor to cool.

If the button pops again every time, pressing it harder is not the fix. Repeated trips mean a jam you have not found or a worn motor, and that is the signal to call a plumber.

Tracing a Leak to Its Source

Leaks come from one of three places, and the location tells you the fix. Dry the cabinet floor, put a paper towel under the unit, run water for a minute, then run the disposal and check again.

Where the water shows up.
Wet spotWhat failedTypical fix
Top, around the sink flange.Putty or gasket under the sink flange has dried.Remove the unit, reseat the flange with fresh putty.
Side, at the dishwasher hose.Loose clamp or cracked drain hose.Tighten or replace the hose and clamp.
Side, at the drain pipe joint.Loose slip nut or worn gasket.Snug the nut or replace the gasket.
Bottom or body of the unit.Cracked housing or failed internal seal.Replace the disposal.

A drip from the body is the one leak that rarely gets a repair. Once the housing or lower seal fails, water is reaching the motor. A loose hose or drain connection is a small job by comparison.

If you find water under the sink and cannot tell where it started, call (503) 974-3329 and describe what you see.

What Should Never Go Down There

A disposal handles soft scraps well and fibrous or sticky material poorly. If you would not want it stuck in a pipe, do not push it through a motor.

  • Grease, fat and oil, which coat the drain and harden further down the line.
  • Celery, corn husks and onion skins, whose strings wrap around the plate.
  • Rice, pasta and oatmeal, which swell with water and turn to paste.
  • Coffee grounds in quantity, which pack into the trap like wet sand.
  • Glass, metal, bottle caps, fruit pits and large bones, which jam or chip the lugs.
  • Paint, solvents and harsh chemical cleaners.

Run cold water before, during and for about fifteen seconds after grinding. Cold water keeps any fat solid enough to be carried away, while hot water lets it smear on the pipe wall.

Repair or Replace: Reading the Numbers

Cost guides help frame the decision, though your own price depends on the unit, the access under your sink and what else turns up. According to HomeGuide, disposal repair runs about $100 to $250. Angi puts a new disposal installed at roughly $200 to $625 and an average near $550.

When the Problem Is Past the Disposal

A disposal that runs fine but leaves the sink full of water has a different problem. The clog sits in the trap, the branch drain or sometimes the line serving the kitchen stack. Running the disposal harder will not clear it, and pouring chemicals in only adds risk.

Kitchen drains clog with grease and soap scum, and the blockage often sits a few feet downstream. Our drain cleaning service handles that, and Angi lists kitchen sink drain work at about $110 to $215.

Knowing When to Stop and Call

Leave it unplugged and call if any of these apply. The hex key spins but the motor still hums on restart. The reset keeps tripping. Water is leaking from the housing. You smell something burning. The sink is draining badly after the disposal is cleared. Or you simply cannot get a good look into the chamber.

The same hex key and reset steps work on a disposal in Cornelius or Hillsboro. Cut power first, keep your hands out, and if the fault is unclear, call (503) 974-3329 before you spend money on parts.

Forest Grove Note: What Your Kitchen Drain Feeds

Forest Grove's sewer lines are split between owners. The City maintains collection lines smaller than 24 inches and all manholes, while Clean Water Services handles trunk lines, lift stations and the Fernhill Treatment Facility. The private lateral, running from your house to the public sewer connection, belongs to you under City Ordinance 52.03.

That is why grease matters. Clean Water Services asks residents not to pour grease down drains and not to flush wipes, even ones labeled flushable, paper towels or mop refills. Kitchen habits matter here, and the Freeze the Grease kit line is 503-681-3678.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Forest Grove sewer policy change what I can put in my disposal?

The City and Clean Water Services ask residents to keep grease out of drains and wipes out of toilets, and your private lateral is your responsibility under City Ordinance 52.03. Scrape greasy pans into the trash.

Is it safe to reach into a jammed disposal?

No. Switch it off at the wall, then unplug it or flip its breaker before you do anything. Even then, use tongs or pliers instead of your fingers, because the plate can still pinch.

Why does my disposal hum but not spin?

A hum with no spin means the motor has power but the plate is stuck. A spoon, bone fragment or compacted scraps are the usual cause. Turn off power, free it with a hex key at the bottom socket, then press the red reset button once the motor has cooled.
