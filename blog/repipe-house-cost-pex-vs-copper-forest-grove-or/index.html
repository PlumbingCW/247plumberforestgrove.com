Repipe House Cost: PEX vs Copper in Forest Grove, OR

By the Forest Grove 24/7 Plumbing team. Updated October 15, 2026.

Cost guides put a whole-house repipe anywhere from about $2,000 to $16,000, and the pipe material is the biggest reason why.

Call (503) 974-3329

Repiping a house in Forest Grove costs less with PEX than with copper. Cost guides such as Angi put the national average for a whole-house repipe near $7,500 and a wide range of $1,500 to $15,000. HomeGuide, pricing a 2,000 square foot home, lists PEX at $2,000 to $4,000, and copper at $8,000 to $16,000.

Those are national guide figures, not a quote. What you pay depends on the house, the access and the material. This guide explains what drives the gap, how long the work takes, when a permit is required, and how to decide whether your pipes need replacing at all. PEX and Copper Side by Side

PEX is flexible plastic tubing. It bends around corners, runs through walls in long lengths and needs fewer joints. Copper is rigid metal pipe that is cut, fitted and soldered at every turn. That difference in labor explains most of the price gap.

The 50-year figure is a test criterion, not a promise about your house. It tells you how the tubing performed under laboratory conditions. Installation quality, water chemistry and how the system is supported matter as much as the pipe itself.

Signs the Pipes Are Due for Replacement

A single leak does not mean a repipe. One pinhole in an otherwise sound line can be repaired. The picture changes when the leaks keep coming, and each repair opens another wall. Plumbers look for patterns, not single events.

  • Pinhole leaks or stains that show up in several rooms over a short period.
  • Rusty or brown water from hot and cold taps even after the line runs a while.
  • Low pressure at fixtures that used to have plenty.
  • Metal pipe that is crusted, flaking or green at the joints.
  • A water bill that climbs with no change in how you use water.

Before blaming the pipes, rule out simpler causes. EPA WaterSense says ideal home water pressure runs 45 to 60 psi, and pressure much lower than 45 may mean a leak. A pressure gauge on a hose bibb takes a minute. A partly closed main valve or a failing pressure regulator can mimic bad pipe.

What Pushes the Price Up or Down

Cost guides show how wide the range is. Angi's $1,500 to $15,000 span covers a small home with open access at one end and a large house with finished walls at the other. Here is what a plumber weighs when pricing it.

Forest Grove Housing Age and Your Pipes

Housing age is the strongest local clue. American Community Survey data for 2020 to 2024 gives Forest Grove a median year built of 1982 across 9,094 housing units. About 48 percent, or 4,376 homes, were built before 1980. That includes 1,030 from 1939 or earlier, 696 from the 1950s, 766 from the 1960s and 1,586 from the 1970s.

Pipe materials have shifted over those decades. Older houses can have galvanized steel, which corrodes from the inside and narrows the pipe, or older copper that has aged. A home built in 1975 may still carry its original supply lines. Age alone is not proof, so a plumber checks the actual pipe.

One thing the City says helps here. Its latest water quality report states it found no lead or galvanized service lines needing replacement. That says nothing about the pipes inside your house. Ask Public Works at 503-992-3258 who handles the service line in your case.

Why Hidden Pipe Brings a Permit

Oregon Building Codes Division guidance says a permit is required to alter piping inside walls, ceilings and floors. A repipe does exactly that. The person doing the work, whether homeowner or contractor, pulls the permit. Forest Grove's Community Development office issues building and plumbing permits and can be reached at 503-992-3229.

Emergency repairs are covered too. If freeze damage or a leak in concealed piping means new pipe longer than five feet, a permit applies. A permit also expires if work does not start within 180 days, so it pays to schedule the job once the paperwork is in.

Two nearby towns handle this differently. In Hillsboro, the city issues its own plumbing permits through Development Services at 150 E Main St. In Banks, plumbing permits come from Washington County, after the city grants land use approval.

How Long a Whole-House Repipe Takes

Angi and Bob Vila both describe the timeline as a few days for a small home to several weeks for a large one. That spread depends on the number of fixtures, wall access and when inspections can be scheduled. Water is shut off for part of each working day, so plan around it.

  1. The plumber walks the house, locates every fixture and plans the new routes.
  2. The permit is issued and the work is scheduled.
  3. Old lines are shut down and new ones are run, usually room by room.
  4. Pressure is tested at each stage and fixtures are reconnected.
  5. An inspection is requested, and wall and ceiling repairs follow.

Expect noise, dust and short windows without water. Move valuables away from walls that will be opened. If the job is large, ask whether the crew can work in sections so one bathroom stays usable.

Do You Really Need a Repipe

Plenty of houses never need one. A plumber can sometimes replace a failed section, add a pressure regulator or swap corroded shutoffs and get years of use. Whether that makes sense depends on how much of the system is the same age and the same material.

When several lines are affected, a full job is usually the cheaper route. Our repiping service covers whole-house work, and if one line is leaking now, leak detection can find where before anyone cuts into a wall. To talk through your own house, call (503) 974-3329.

Forest Grove Note: Pipe Age, Permits and Water Sources

Forest Grove is the oldest city in Washington County, incorporated in 1872, and the housing stock shows it. Beyond the 1,030 homes from 1939 or earlier, the National Register Clark and Painter's Woods Historic Districts south of downtown add older homes, and Painter's Woods covers residential development from 1880 to 1948. A repipe in a house like that is as much a carpentry project as a plumbing one.

The City runs its own water utility from Clear Creek Watershed and the Joint Water Commission. Call City Public Works at 503-992-3258 for anything at the meter. Do not shut water off at the meter yourself. Use the main valve at the house.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a permit to repipe a house in Forest Grove?

Yes. Oregon guidance requires a permit to alter piping inside walls, ceilings or floors, and a repipe does that. Forest Grove Community Development issues plumbing permits at 503-992-3229. Whoever does the work pulls the permit, so a licensed plumber normally handles it. A permit lapses if work does not begin within 180 days.

Is PEX or copper cheaper to install?

PEX, by a wide margin in published cost guides. HomeGuide's figures for a 2,000 square foot home show PEX at $2,000 to $4,000, and copper at $8,000 to $16,000. The gap comes mainly from labor. Your own price depends on the house, access and fixtures, and we give a clear price before work starts.

How long does a whole-house repipe take?

Angi and Bob Vila describe it as a few days for a small home to several weeks for a large one. Timing depends on fixture count, wall access and inspection scheduling. Water is off for part of each working day. Ask the plumber for a written schedule so you know which rooms lose water and when.

Will a repipe damage my walls?

Some opening is usually needed, though PEX often needs less because it bends through small access holes. Plumbers cut inspection openings, then patch them. Ask whether drywall repair and paint are part of the bid or handled separately, since that can change the real cost of the project.
